Give each entity a durable claim naming the session allowed to mutate it, so two driving First Officers sharing one state checkout cannot select, dispatch, or mutate the same entity.

## Problem

`docs/dev/README.md:47` declares that while a sprint is actively driven, other FO sessions sharing the state checkout "stay out of its members", and that ownership is coordinated out-of-band "until a sprint carries a machine-readable owner". It names `xp` as the graduation trigger, but `xp` is scoped to an FO-Commander message transport and carries no notion of mutation authority, acquisition, or takeover — so no task currently owns the guarantee, and `xp` is unscored.

The advisory form is demonstrably insufficient. On 2026-07-25 a second FO booted into this workflow while the Commander was actively driving, found `state ready` blocked by an uncommitted stage report, and committed and pushed sprint member `first-officer-gate-command-lifecycle`'s in-flight cycle-34 report. The rule existed and was unambiguous; nothing in the boot path surfaced either the rule or the live peer. `status --boot` reported `team_state` for the booting session's own team and a local `pr:` mirror; neither indicates that another session holds the entity. Peer commits landed on the state branch minutes after the intrusion, confirming the owner was live throughout.

Two adjacent tasks touch the area without covering it: `sy` defines captain-authorized active scope within one session, and `mzk` asks for ownership tracing before acting on shared GitHub state. Neither supplies a durable, machine-checked owner for an entity.

## Proposed approach

These captain rulings from the 2026-07-25 shaping conversation bind the design:

- The claim is keyed to the **entity and session**, not the sprint. A sprint is a query whose membership is generated by its own review rounds — all five current durable-decisions members were filed mid-sprint — so a sprint-keyed claim leaves membership undefined exactly when the sprint is most active. A newly filed entity is unclaimed until claimed.
- Workstream or sprint selection is a **policy layered over the primitive**, not a second primitive. Claiming everything matching a status query is a loop over per-entity acquisition. Auto-claiming successors a review round spawns is captain-authorized, never silent.
- Enforcement starts at **two points**: boot surfaces the claim, and the state-mutation seam refuses a non-owner. Additional guard points are added only when one of these proves insufficient — a new standing enforcement process is the last resort under the working principles.
- Expiry **marks a claim stale and never auto-steals**. Takeover is captain-authorized and per entity.
- Acquisition should ride the existing state publisher rather than introduce a second writer. A git-backed shared checkout already provides compare-and-swap: acquisition is a fast-forward-only commit, and a conflicting acquirer loses the push and re-reads to name the current owner. Note `sync-merge-guard-archive-state` is concurrently building `internal/statesync` with exactly non-fast-forward detection and re-push.
- Owner identity already exists per host and is already read by the binary: `dispatch reconcile` matches `leadSessionId` against `$CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`; Codex exposes `CODEX_THREAD_ID` and Pi `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`. Only the claim epoch is new.
- The claim is a per-entity durable fact and does not depend on `sy` shipping first; the two may land independently.

### Skeleton carve (captain-approved re-carve, 2026-07-26)

This task is the sprint's walking skeleton: the thinnest mechanism that makes the multi-FO journey observable, with everything else held fixed. It ships exactly three things — **acquire** (`state claim <slug>` writes `claim-session`), **disclose** (boot surfaces a peer's claim: slug and owner identity), and **refuse** (one mutation door refuses a non-owner and names the owner) — with the live two-session run as the definition of done. Every hardening edge designed in earlier cycles is deferred intact, not rejected, to `ppf` (`claim-mechanism-hardening`, id `ppfr0qqafmaqmhy3kyj418dw`), which carries each edge together with the condition the skeleton holds fixed. The deferral rule applied to each: an edge is deferrable exactly when the skeleton's journey holds its triggering condition fixed — one acquirer at a time (no race, so no lost-race rollback and no divergent-placement split-brain), a short journey with a live owner (no expiry lease), a journey that ends at the refusal (no takeover or release), one host (no cross-host identity ambiguity), and one exercised door (no guard at the remaining doors). None of those conditions can break inside the skeleton's own journey.

### Storage decision: one flat frontmatter field on the entity

The skeleton stores the claim as one flat scalar frontmatter field on the claimed entity itself:

```yaml
claim-session: claude:1db707f2-…        # <host>:<value> — host-prefixed session identity
```

Captain annotations (2026-07-26) on this record: the storage shape is agreed with no stage granularity — the claim is per entity, never per (entity, stage). The `claim-expiry` owner-renewed lease from the second annotation is designed but defers to `ppf` with the rest of the hardening; the skeleton's journey is short and its owner stays alive throughout, so the skeleton carries no expiry field at all.

Alternatives considered, and why each is insufficient:

- **A central claims file** (e.g. `_claims.yaml`) is the simplest single object, but every acquisition by every session mutates the same path. Concurrent acquisitions of *different* entities would then contend or conflict on one file, destroying the disjoint-path property that makes `pull --rebase` safe for peers today, and creating a serialization hotspot exactly where the design needs per-entity independence. It is also a second source of truth that can drift from the entity (an archived entity's row lingering as a live claim).
- **A per-entity sidecar object** (e.g. `_claims/<slug>`) preserves disjoint paths but is a second object whose lifecycle — create, flat-vs-folder form, rename, archive — must mirror the entity's forever. Every guard would resolve two paths, and drift between them is a new failure mode. Frontmatter travels with the entity through archive automatically, and every mutation door already parses frontmatter before acting.
- **A nested `claim:` mapping** matches `gates:` stylistically, but `gates:` needed a dedicated yaml.Node writer with byte-preservation invariants to be safe. Two flat scalars ride the existing flat-field frontmatter machinery (`ParseFrontmatter`, the `--set` writer) with no new parser surface.

The claim does not become a second writer: acquisition writes the entity file through the existing frontmatter writer, commits path-scoped with the literal-pathspec discipline `rd` shipped, and publishes through `internal/statesync.Publish` — the one state publisher. `docs/schema/entity.mdschema.yml` gains `claim-session` as an optional canonical entry.

### Session identity

One helper serializes the current session identity from the host env the binary already reads (`dispatch reconcile` matches `leadSessionId` against `$CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`; Codex exposes `CODEX_THREAD_ID`; Pi `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`): `claude:<id>` / `codex:<id>` / `pi:<dir>`. Owner match is exact string equality. A process with none of the three markers has no identity: it can mutate unclaimed entities exactly as today, but a claimed entity refuses it like any non-owner. The host prefix keeps refusal messages actionable and disambiguates value shapes across hosts. A bare unprefixed value was considered and is insufficient: a Pi directory and a Claude UUID are indistinguishable in refusals and logs.

Cross-host marker ambiguity (the recorded nested-Claude-under-Codex mis-detection) defers to `ppf` under the skeleton's one-host condition — its journey runs both sessions on a single host, where the markers cannot conflict. `ppf` carries the requirement already designed here: identity resolution must share the one existing ambiguity policy of `internal/dispatch/build.go:254-281` (refuse and ask for `--host` when markers for different hosts are simultaneously set) rather than invent a second, divergent one.

### Acquisition: `spacedock state claim <slug>`

Acquisition lives under `state`, beside `init|commit|ready`, because it is a state-checkout commit-and-publish verb, not a field edit. Skeleton protocol:

1. Resolve the entity in active scope (archived refuses, as mutation resolution already does).
2. `statesync.Pull` to current, then read the claim. Claimed by another session → refuse, naming the owner. Claimed by self → no-op.
3. Write `claim-session` at the canonical frontmatter position (fixed placement is load-bearing — see spike), path-scoped commit.
4. `statesync.Publish`. `pushed`/`no-op` → acquired; any other outcome exits non-zero reporting the publisher's result.

Deferred to `ppf` with the rest of the hardening, under the one-acquirer-at-a-time condition the skeleton's journey holds fixed: lost-race detection and rollback on a halted publish, `--release`, the `--take --from <owner>` captain-authorized takeover CAS, reserved-field refusal of direct `--set claim-*` writes, and duplicate-claim fail-closed reads.

Simplest alternative to the verb: hand-rolled `status --set claim-session=…` plus `state commit`. Insufficient even for the skeleton: the claim-read-before-write and the owner-naming refusal would live in FO prose — exactly the class of manual multi-step discipline the 2026-07-25 breach came from — and the fixed-position write the spike proved load-bearing would be unenforced.

Sprint/workstream claiming stays a policy loop over this per-entity verb, captain-authorized in the FO contract text — never a batch primitive in the binary.

### Enforcement point 1: boot surfaces peer claims

`status --boot` (and its `--json` form) gains a CLAIMS disclosure listing every active entity claimed by a session other than the booting one: slug and owner identity. No age, no expiry state, no rendering grammar — those defer to `ppf` with the lease. This is precisely the disclosure whose absence let the breach FO act blind: the fixture reproducing that boot now shows the live peer. Alternative considered: leave the FO to grep frontmatter — insufficient, because the breach demonstrates a cold boot does not go looking; disclosure must ride the boot record the FO already reads.

The `claim-expiry` owner-renewed lease (EXPIRED marking, renewal obligation, dead-session self-declaration — designed in cycle 2 under the captain's expiry annotation) defers to `ppf` under the short-journey/live-owner condition. The captain ruling that expiry marks and never auto-steals now binds `ppf` rather than this task.

### Enforcement point 2: one mutation door refuses a non-owner

One guard function (claim parse + identity compare + owner-naming refusal) is called at ONE door: the `resolveMutationEntity` call site (internal/status/native_runner.go:533), which the journey exercises through `status --set`. That single call site is the smallest possible cut — `merge guard` and the archive path route through the same resolver and inherit the refusal at zero added code, which is one seam, not extra doors. The genuinely separate doors — `state commit <slug>` target resolution (the breach's actual vector) and the `gate record`/`consume` paths — defer to `ppf` under the one-exercised-door condition; the FO contract's routing keeps the journey inside the guarded door until then.

The refusal names the current owner; its exact format is deliberately unspecified in the skeleton. The owning session and unclaimed entities pass unchanged — the guard cannot over-fire on today's flows. Known residual, accepted: raw `git` in the state checkout bypasses any binary guard (ensign stage-report commits use it by design, under the dispatching FO's authority). The claim defends rule-following sessions against accidental collision; the contract already routes FO state writes through the binary.

## Spike: two-writer claim CAS over real Git (2026-07-26)

Riskiest mechanism exercised first, per the backlog gate: a bare origin (`spacedock-state/dev`) plus two clones, racing real claim commits on one seeded entity. Three scenarios, all against stock git:

1. **Same-position race (the designed shape).** A and B both insert `claim-session`/`claim-at` at the canonical position (after `issue:`); A commit `8ebcb06` pushes first; B commit `a6411f6` push is rejected non-fast-forward ("fetch first"); B's `pull --rebase` CONFLICTS with `task.md` unmerged; `rebase --abort` restores exactly `a6411f6`; `reset --hard HEAD~1` drops the claim commit; re-pull is clean and B reads `claim-session: claude:aaaa-1111-session` — the winner, by name. Origin history holds exactly one claim commit. The full acquire/lose/recover/report loop works with zero new git machinery.
2. **Divergent-position race (hazard found).** A inserts the claim after `issue:`, B after `title:`. B's `pull --rebase` AUTOMERGES CLEANLY and pushes a split-brain entity carrying **two** `claim-session` lines to origin. Textual conflict is therefore NOT guaranteed by git — it is a property the writer must manufacture. Two design consequences are load-bearing: the claim writer always places the fields at one canonical fixed position, and every claim reader fails closed on duplicated claim fields instead of letting YAML last-key-wins silently pick a winner.
3. **Owner activity vs. acquisition of an unclaimed entity.** A pushes an ordinary `status:` change; B's claim insertion rebases cleanly over it and publishes. Disjoint-field edits do not spuriously conflict, so peers' ordinary work never blocks legitimate acquisition, and the guard (checked before writing) is what protects a *claimed* entity — the CAS only has to arbitrate claim-vs-claim writes, which scenario 1 proves it does.

Spike artifacts were throwaway; the scenario-1 and scenario-2 shapes seed the implementation's first tests. Mechanisms relied on without further spiking, already proven in-repo by `rd`: `statesync.Publish/Pull/Preflight` conflict discovery and abort-with-evidence, literal-pathspec path-scoped staging, and the two-clone real-Git test harness. Under the skeleton carve, scenario 2's split-brain hazard is deferred to `ppf` under the no-concurrent-write condition — the skeleton has one acquirer at a time, so divergent-placement racing writes cannot arise inside its journey; this section stands as the recorded evidence that the deferral is informed, and as `ppf`'s primary design input.

## Out of scope

- A cross-session FO-Commander message channel — that is `xp`.
- Captain-authorized active-scope semantics within a single session — that is `sy`.
- A merge claim serializing writes to product `main`. Different lifetime and blast radius; keep it separate and note `mzk` as adjacent.
- Intra-session worker coordination, which `SendMessage` and the roster already cover.
- Stage-level claim granularity. Captain ruling on this design record (2026-07-26): the claim is per entity, never per (entity, stage). Do not reintroduce a per-stage claim as an "obvious extension" — a stage handoff within one session needs no claim, and a cross-session handoff is a release/acquire (or captain-authorized takeover) of the whole entity.
- Filtering `--next`/dispatchable output by peer claims, auto-claim at dispatch, or any third enforcement point — deferred until boot disclosure plus mutation refusal prove insufficient, per the two-point ruling.
- Guarding raw-git writes to the state checkout; the binary can only guard its own doors.
- The hardening edges deferred to `ppf` (`claim-mechanism-hardening`, id `ppfr0qqafmaqmhy3kyj418dw`), each carried there with the condition the skeleton holds fixed: concurrent-acquisition lost-race rollback; duplicate-claim fail-closed reads; the `claim-expiry` lease with EXPIRED marking (the never-auto-steal ruling binds `ppf`); `--release` and `--take --from <owner>` takeover; multi-host identity and marker-ambiguity refusal; the remaining mutation doors (`state commit`, `gate record`/`consume`); reserved `claim-*` refusal under `--set`; and the refusal message's exact rendering grammar.

## Acceptance criteria

The skeleton carries four ACs. The five hardening ACs from cycles 1–3 (concurrent acquisition, expiry lease, takeover/release, binary-managed claim fields, multi-host identity) moved intact to `ppf`, each with the condition the skeleton holds fixed.

**AC-1 (VALUE) — A non-owning session's mutation of a claimed entity fails at the guarded door, and neither the entity nor origin moves.**
Verified by: a two-clone real-Git fixture where session A claims an entity and a distinct injected session identity runs `status --set status=…` against it, asserting non-zero exit, byte-identical entity file, and unmoved origin ref. Baseline that can move the wrong way: the same leg against the pre-change binary succeeds — the mutation lands and publishes, the breach's shape — and the deliverable turns that success into a refusal. Over-fire legs: the owner's identical mutation and any session's mutation of an unclaimed entity still exit 0. Falsified by: deleting the guard call at the `resolveMutationEntity` call site flips the leg back to success.

**AC-2 — The refusal names the current owner.**
Verified by: the AC-1 refusal leg additionally asserts stderr contains the owner's host-prefixed session identity (`claude:<id>`-form). The exact message format is deliberately unspecified — the assertion is on the owner identity's presence, nothing more. Falsified by: a refusal that does not carry the owner's identity.

**AC-3 — A cold boot in a checkout with peer-claimed entities surfaces each peer claim.**
Verified by: a fixture reproducing the breach's boot blindness — entity claimed by session A, boot run under session B — asserting the boot output (prose and `--json`) names the slug and A's identity; a boot with no peer claims shows none. Falsified by: removing the claims scan from `gatherBoot`.

**AC-4 (LIVE, DoD) — An engineered cross-session collision between two real host sessions is refused on the live front door, with durable evidence.**
Verified by: one shared runtime scenario (`claim-collision`) in the existing `internal/ensigncycle` live machinery — no second harness, no session orchestrator, no fixture standing in for the claim mechanism. The scenario's runner makes two sequential real launches against one fixture workflow: launch 1 (session A) claims and drives an entity, its real host-read session identity landing durably in `claim-session`; launch 2 (session B) cold-boots, must surface A's claim in its boot output, then DELIBERATELY attempts a mutation of A's entity and is refused. The collision is engineered, not incidental — two sessions coexisting without touching each other's entities proves nothing. Evidence is durable, not observed: the preserved stream/final-message artifacts carry the refusal naming A's identity, and the post-run entity frontmatter is byte-identical to A's last write. Baseline that can move the wrong way: the same scenario against the pre-change binary SUCCEEDS in mutating A's entity — literally the recorded 2026-07-25 breach — and the offline negative case in `shared_scenarios_negative_test.go` builds that breached end-state and proves the assertion goes red on it. Falsified by: removing the guard call at the one guarded door, which flips launch 2 back to a successful mutation and lets the run complete. Per the shared-scenario parity guard, the scenario ships with Claude and Codex runner entries plus a Pi coverage entry.
